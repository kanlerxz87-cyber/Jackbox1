Privacy &amp; Anonymisation

What is published, what never leaves the private workspace, and how the split is enforced in code.

Nothing personal is on this site, and nothing personal can be The subject property is described as “a single-tenant Jack in the Box under a triple-net lease in central Los Angeles”. That is the entire level of specificity. No address, APN, owner name, tenant entity name, lease term, rent figure, loan balance, tax return detail or adviser name appears anywhere in this repository's published output.

The rule that governs this project

Personal and asset-identifying information is the highest-priority constraint, above completeness. When the two conflict, the site stays incomplete. That is why fourteen rows are blank rather than filled: filling them would have required publishing, or inferring from, information that belongs in a private file.

How anonymisation is enforced technically

  1. Generator-level separation. The build script reads only data/ and writes only docs/. The private/ directory is in .gitignore and is never copied.
  2. Placeholders in the private templates. The lease abstract and notice calendar ship as empty templates with [[PLACEHOLDER]] fields, so no real term can be committed by accident.
  3. Registry validation. The build fails if a row marked verified has no source link — which makes “verified” mean “linked”, not “remembered”.
  4. No client-side collection at all. No analytics, no cookies, no localStorage, no network requests from the calculator, no external fonts or scripts. The suggestion tool writes to a textarea you can copy and nothing else.
  5. Static hosting only. GitHub Pages serves files; there is no server to log a visitor, and no form to submit.

Handling rules for the private workspace

  • Access control. Single-tenant, encrypted at rest, with per-user accounts and an access log. No shared logins.
  • Named advisers only. Share under NDA with a written list of who holds access and why.
  • Minimisation. The private workspace holds only what the decision needs — not full personal financial statements if a summary will do.
  • Retention. Set a review date and a deletion date for each sensitive document.
  • No re-identification in aggregate outputs. Anything published must be unable to point at one parcel or one person. A single-asset site is inherently borderline; the mitigation is that no identifying facts are included at all.
  • Breach readiness. Know who to notify and how, before it is needed. California's breach-notification statute applies to personal information held by a business.

What the reader of this site can and cannot learn

Can learn

  • How the tax machinery works at these rates, with primary-law links.
  • That the tenant's domestic system has been shrinking, from its own filings.
  • Which official databases answer which hazard question, and how to query them.
  • What the decision gates are and why they matter.

Cannot learn

  • Where the property is.
  • Who owns it, or what they paid.
  • What the rent is, or when the lease expires more precisely than 2030–2035.
  • What the owner's tax position is.
  • What the property is worth.
This is not legal advice on privacy compliance The handling rules above are operational practice for this workspace. California's CCPA/CPRA and the breach-notification statute impose specific obligations on a business holding personal information; confirm the applicable duties with counsel rather than relying on this page.
