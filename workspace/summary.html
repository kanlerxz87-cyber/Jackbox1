Jack in the Box · NNN leasehold · central Los Angeles — advisor brief, prepared 2026-11-25. Anonymised: no address, APN, owner or lease terms are included.

1 · The question

An owner holds a single-tenant Jack in the Box on a triple-net lease in central Los Angeles. The lease expires somewhere between 2030 and 2035. The owner must decide whether to sell before expiry or to pursue a renewal, weighing after-tax proceeds against the risk and opportunity of holding, including low-probability events that would damage the local economy.

3 · What is missing

Five gaps block a defensible decision: (1) no parcel identifier, so no hazard zone, zoning, traffic count or assessed value; (2) no lease document, so no expiration, rent, escalator or option terms; (3) no verified comparable sales or quotes, so no short-lease discount and no capitalisation rate; (4) no basis or depreciation history, so no after-tax result; (5) no defined trade area, so the city and county figures on this site are substitutes rather than findings.

4 · What the evidence suggests, stated as a direction rather than a verdict

  1. The renewal case must be made to the operator, and early. The parent has been shrinking its domestic estate and divesting toward an asset-light model. Franchisees decide renewals on unit economics, and they decide them well before expiry.
  2. Selling is more expensive than the headline price implies. At these rates, tax and transfer cost can consume roughly a fifth or more of a sale price, with Measure ULA alone worth 4–5.5% in the City.
  3. The regional economy does not rescue a weak lease. Flat office employment, shrinking information and manufacturing employment, a city income base below the county, and a near-5% ten-year rate are all headwinds to value.
  4. The dominant unknown is the short-lease discount. Until it is priced from recorded sales, the sell case cannot be compared to the hold case at all.
  5. Neither option is comfortable. Holding concentrates capital in one asset in one seismic region; selling converts a deferred tax into a paid one and forfeits any renewal upside. That tension is the decision.

5 · What to do next, in order

  1. Open the private workspace and place the deed, tax bill and lease in it (a day).
  2. Run the four hazard lookups and geocode the parcel (an afternoon).
  3. Abstract the lease and reconcile the notice calendar with counsel (a week).
  4. Request three broker opinions and three insurance quotes (two weeks).
  5. Have the adviser reconstruct basis, recapture and entity-level tax (two weeks, in parallel).
  6. Re-run the calculator with real inputs, then declare a decision and record it with its rationale.
