Questions &amp; Gaps

Thirty questions, ranked. Each with the specific document or database that answers it.

How to use this page Each row is a question that must be answered before a sell-or-renew decision can be defended. The middle column is how to obtain the answer — deliberately concrete, because “get more data” is not an instruction. Nothing on this page is a data claim, so nothing here needs a source link; the sourced findings live on the sector pages.
Blocking and non-blocking questions. Priority reflects how much the answer can move the final decision.
IDQuestionPriorityHow to obtain the answerWhy it matters
Q-01 Identify the parcel
What is the street address, APN, and legal description of the subject property?		 highest Supply the APN from the recorded deed or the county tax bill. Without it, hazard zone status, zoning, tax rate area, traffic counts and every comparable are unreachable. 14 rows stay blank. F-01
Q-02 Pin the lease term
What is the exact expiration date, and what is the notice window for exercising each renewal option?		 highest Read the lease. Then reconcile against the notice calendar in the private workspace. The whole sell-versus-renew case keys off this date; a five-year uncertainty band spans an entire market cycle. F-02
Q-03 Establish rent and escalation
What is current base rent, the escalation mechanism (fixed bumps, CPI, or fair-market reset), and what does the tenant reimburse?		 highest Abstract the lease: base rent, escalators, NNN recoveries, caps on controllable CAM. Determines the income the buyer is paying for and whether a renewal is priced off the old rent or the market. F-02
Q-04 Read the renewal options
How many options, how long each, who holds them, and is renewal rent fixed, indexed, or negotiated?		 highest Abstract the options clause verbatim. A fixed-rent option at below-market terms is a liability; a market-reset option is an asset. Opposite conclusions. F-02
Q-05 Confirm the tenant entity
Is the tenant Jack in the Box Inc., a franchisee entity, or a franchisee sublease? Who actually signs a renewal?		 high Match the named tenant on the lease to SEC filings and to the operator's franchise disclosure where available. The parent is divesting and going asset-light; a franchisee's balance sheet is a different credit.
Q-06 Get the assessed value and tax basis
What is the current assessed value, the Proposition 13 base-year value, and the date of the last change of ownership?		 highest Pull the Assessor's parcel record and the most recent secured tax bill. Drives both the property-tax line and the recapture calculation on a sale. F-04
Q-07 Quantify the depreciation taken
How much depreciation has been claimed, under which schedule, and how much is unrecaptured §1250 gain?		 highest Reconstruct from filed returns with the tax adviser; carry-forward schedules are required. At a 25% recapture ceiling this is usually the largest single tax line in a sale. F-04
Q-08 Fix the ownership entity and the owner's bracket
Is the property held personally, in an LLC taxed as a partnership, or in a corporation — and at what marginal rate does a gain land?		 high Confirm with the adviser; model entity-level tax if it is a C corporation. Entity form can change the after-tax answer more than the sale price does. F-04
Q-09 Get comparable sale evidence
What have comparable net-lease QSR assets in central Los Angeles actually traded at, at what cap rate, and with how much term remaining?		 highest Pull recorded sales from the county recorder and confirm terms with the brokers involved. The short-lease discount cannot be priced without this. No number is published until it exists. F-03
Q-10 Price the short-lease discount
How much value per year of remaining term is the market actually charging in this submarket?		 highest Regress or pair-match recorded sales on remaining term; sanity-check against live quotes. This is the single largest driver of value between now and 2030. F-03
Q-11 Verify the hazard zones at the parcel
Is the parcel in a Very High Fire Hazard Severity Zone, an Alquist-Priolo fault zone, a liquefaction zone, or a FEMA flood zone?		 high Run the APN through CAL FIRE FHSZ, CGS EQ Zapp and FEMA NFHL, and save the dated screenshots. Affects insurance cost, lender appetite, and disclosure obligations on a sale. F-01
Q-12 Get current insurance quotes
What would a replacement NNN policy cost today, and what is excluded (shake, flood, wildfire)?		 high Request three quotes using the actual address and construction type. Insurance availability, not price, is what has been killing California commercial deals.
Q-13 Define the trade area properly
Which Census tracts actually form this restaurant's trade area — drive-time, not ZIP, not city?		 high Geocode the parcel, then build a 3/5/10-minute drive-time ring and list the intersecting tracts. City and county figures are substitutes; a QSR lives and dies inside roughly a 3-mile ring. F-05
Q-14 Test the co-tenancy and access assumptions
What else is on the hard corner or in the centre, what are the access and signal conditions, and is there a drive-thru queue constraint?		 medium Site visit with photographs; pull Caltrans or LADOT counts for the adjacent arterial. Drive-thru throughput is the revenue engine; a median or a queuing problem can cap sales.
Q-15 Check the zoning and any overlays
What is the zoning, is the use legal-conforming, and are there specific-plan or overlay restrictions on the site?		 medium ZIMAS query plus the applicable community plan. Matters for a re-tenanting or redevelopment exit if the tenant leaves.
Q-16 Establish the exit alternative
If the tenant vacates, what could the site become, at what cost, and with what entitlement risk?		 high Preliminary feasibility with a local architect; confirm the zoning envelope. The floor under the sell decision is the alternate-use value, not the vacant-building value.
Q-17 Confirm the sale cost stack
What are the actual brokerage, legal, escrow, title and transfer-tax costs at the modelled price?		 high Get a title/escrow estimate and confirm the City of Los Angeles transfer-tax and Measure ULA treatment in writing. Measure ULA alone is 4% above $5m, which is a decision-changing number. F-04
Q-18 Decide on a 1031 exchange posture
If a sale happens, is a like-kind exchange intended, and is replacement property identified in advance?		 medium Engage a qualified intermediary before closing, not after. Deferral can dominate the after-tax arithmetic; it is a timing decision, so it must be made first.
Q-19 Fix the hold-case discount rate
What discount rate and terminal cap rate should the hold case use?		 medium Derive from the 10-year Treasury plus a net-lease spread, and disclose both. Holding is only comparable to selling when both are discounted at the same rate.
Q-20 Establish the tenant's unit-level economics
What does this unit actually sell, and what is its four-wall margin?		 high Request the tenant's sales figures under the lease's reporting clause, or the franchisee's Item 19 where published. A renewal is a business decision by the operator; without sales, neither side can price it.
Q-21 Document the black-swan exposure
What happens to rent, insurance and exit liquidity if a wildfire, earthquake or flood event hits the region?		 high Stress the model across the three together, not separately, and confirm business-interruption terms. Loss of rent, repair cost and exit liquidity tend to fail at the same time, not independently.
Q-22 Confirm the seismic retrofit status
Has the building been retrofitted to current standards, and is a retrofit ordinance applicable?		 medium Pull the building permit history and any unreinforced-masonry or soft-story notices. A retrofit obligation arriving mid-lease is a capital call the lease may not allocate.
Q-23 Check for environmental encumbrances
Is there any recognised environmental condition, tank, or dry-cleaner history on or adjacent to the site?		 medium Phase I ESA if not already in hand; check the state's GeoTracker and EnviroStor. A neighbouring release can impair both value and financeability.
Q-24 Identify all notice and consent parties
Does the lender, ground lessor, or any REA require notice or consent on a sale or renewal?		 medium Read the loan documents and any reciprocal easement agreements. Missing a consent can delay or kill a closing.
Q-25 Fix the hold-period horizon
Over what horizon is the owner comparing sell versus hold — 3, 5, 10 years, or through the next option?		 medium Decide explicitly and write it down; the answer changes the ranking. A ten-year hold and a three-year hold rank the same two options differently.
Q-26 Check school and amenity evidence for family trade
Do school assignment and ratings support family-driven daytime and evening traffic?		 low Pull the district's assignment for the address and the state's accountability data. Relevant for a suburban unit; largely irrelevant for a central-LA commercial corridor.
Q-27 Confirm the traffic and access counts
What are the current AADT and peak-hour volumes on the adjacent arterials?		 medium Pull Caltrans or LADOT published counts for the specific segment. Traffic counts are the standard defensible proxy for QSR sales potential. F-01
Q-28 Establish the competitor and cannibalisation set
Which competing QSR and JITB units sit inside the trade area, and is a new unit planned nearby?		 medium Map competitors within the ring; ask the franchisee about protected territory. A new unit inside the ring transfers sales rather than creating them.
Q-29 Reconcile the notice calendar to the lease
Does the private notice calendar agree with the lease on every deadline and cure period?		 high Line-by-line reconciliation, signed off by counsel. A missed option window converts an asset into a vacancy. F-02
Q-30 Prove the data pipeline is reproducible
Can every published row be regenerated from its source on demand?		 medium Re-run the build and re-pull each linked endpoint; diff the output. This is what keeps the site honest as time passes and rates move.

What we already have, and what it can and cannot support

Solid enough to rely on

  • The operator's own filed unit counts, royalties and rental revenue (SEC 10-K, three years).
  • The statutory tax machinery: 1% Prop 13 cap, 2% assessment growth, 39-year straight-line, 25% recapture ceiling, 3.8% NIIT, 12.3% + 1% California top rates.
  • Regional labour market and price levels (BLS), with preliminary flags preserved.
  • Population, income and housing context (ACS 2020–2024 5-year).

Not solid enough to act on

  • Anything parcel-specific: hazard zones, zoning, AADT, tax rate area, assessed value.
  • Anything lease-specific: expiration, rent, escalators, options, notice windows.
  • Anything market-specific: cap rates, comparable sales, the short-lease discount.
  • Anything owner-specific: basis, depreciation history, entity form, financing.
