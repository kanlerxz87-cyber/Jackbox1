What the numbers say, carefully

Supportive

  • Metro leisure and hospitality employment grew 2.0% year over year (LAB-07) — the closest official proxy for restaurant demand.
  • Metro total nonfarm employment grew 0.9% (LAB-06).
  • CPI in the metro is running at roughly 3.3–3.7% over twelve months (PRI-02) — enough to keep a CPI-linked escalator meaningful.

Against

  • City median household income (81,939) sits 9% below the county (90,112) — this is not the affluent side of the region.
  • Information-sector employment is down 1.5% and manufacturing down 1.5% (LAB-09, LAB-10): the daytime office and industrial lunch base is shrinking.
  • Professional and business services grew only 0.5% (LAB-08) — office-using employment is close to flat.
  • The 10-year Treasury at 4.92% (RAT-01) is a headwind for capitalisation-rate compression, which caps how much a buyer will pay for the same rent.
The honest read A flat-to-modestly-growing metro with a below-county-average income base, shrinking office and industrial employment, and a 4.9% ten-year rate is not a story that rescues a weak lease. It is a story in which the lease terms and the tenant's unit economics do all the work.
