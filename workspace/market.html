Why this single number dominates the decision

It is the price of time

A buyer of net-lease paper is buying a bond-like income stream with a known end date. Every year of remaining term is worth something; every year closer to expiry is worth less. The market's price for that decay — usually expressed in basis points of yield per year of term — is the short-lease discount.

It compounds into the exit

If the discount is 25 bps of yield per year of lost term and the asset yields 5.75% with nine years left, the arithmetic on a 2030 expiry is very different from a 2035 expiry. The owner's own uncertainty band — five years — spans most of the difference between “sell now” and “hold”.

It cannot be assumed away

Using a national average short-lease discount would import someone else's market into a central-LA corner site. That is precisely the proxy error W-01 exists to prevent, applied to price instead of to demographics.

What would let us publish a number

  1. Recorded sales. Pull grant deeds for net-lease QSR assets in central Los Angeles from the county recorder for the last 36 months; confirm remaining term at closing and the allocation between real property and intangible/business value.
  2. Written quotes. Ask at least three active net-lease brokers for indicative pricing on the actual asset, with the actual term, in writing.
  3. Pair-matching. Where the same tenant or brand has traded twice with different remaining terms, use the pair to isolate the term effect from the credit effect.
  4. Triangulate. Report a range and the sample size, not a point estimate. If the sample is under roughly five observations, the honest output is “insufficient evidence”, which is what this page says today.
Interim position Until the discount is priced, the defensible posture is to prepare both cases and commit to neither — and to make sure the notice calendar is diarised so that the renewal option is not lost by default. See Sell vs hold and Decision gates.
