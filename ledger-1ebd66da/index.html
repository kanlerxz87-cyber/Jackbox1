Executive overview

Should the parcel be sold before the lease ends, or renewed?

This workspace collects the evidence that a hold-versus-renew decision actually depends on, marks every figure with its official source, and — where a fact is missing — leaves the cell empty and flags it instead of filling it in. It publishes no owner identity, no lease terms and no parcel identifier, because none of those belong on a public page.

The decision cannot be closed with the inputs currently in hand. Four of the five variables that dominate the answer are not in the brief: the exact lease expiry date (a 2030–2035 range was given), the current base rent and escalations, the owner's adjusted basis and depreciation history, and the parcel identity itself. No amount of regional research substitutes for them. This workspace therefore does three things: it verifies what can be verified, it publishes the exact list of what is still missing, and it sequences the work so nothing irreversible happens before those gaps close. The decision on the table

Three paths, and a fourth that should be rejected on its face:

A · Sell before expiry

Convert the leased fee to cash while a credit tenant is in place. The buyer prices remaining term, so value decays as expiry approaches — the short-lease discount is real and it is the whole reason timing matters.

Gated by: basis and depreciation (tax), cap-rate evidence, lease term certainty.

B · Negotiate a renewal, then decide

Trade a shorter term and a lower rent for certainty now, then sell into a longer weighted-average lease term at a tighter cap. Often the highest-value path, and the one that needs the most preparation.

Gated by: renewal option language, tenant's own economics, market rent evidence.

C · Hold to expiry and re-let

Accept vacancy and re-leasing risk in exchange for the option value of a stronger trade area or a redevelopment play. Only rational if the land, not the building, is the asset.

Gated by: zoning, land value, tract-level demand evidence.

Rejected: "decide now on regional data." Regional figures cannot distinguish a parcel on a strong block from one on a weak block, and the Los Angeles County series have just moved in a way that makes naive extrapolation actively misleading — see the finding on the 2025 county population loss below.

What is verified, and what is not

Verified against an official source

  • County and metro population, income, labour force and CPI
  • The tenant's audited financial position and lease-structure benchmarks
  • Federal and California statutory tax rates that apply to a sale
  • The City of Los Angeles transfer-tax schedule, from the ballot text itself
  • That an official parcel-level hazard lookup exists for flood, fire and seismic

Every one of these has a link on Sources.

Left blank and flagged

  • Parcel AIN, area, assessed land/improvement split
  • FEMA flood zone, CGS seismic zone, CAL FIRE hazard zone for the parcel
  • Exact lease expiry, base rent, escalations, options, expense pass-throughs
  • Owner basis, holding period, entity, filing status, material participation
  • Census-tract values — the tract cannot be selected without the address
  • Submarket NNN cap rates — no official statistical series publishes them

Findings — each with the source that supports it

1 · The county's population turned negative in 2025, and the state attributes it partly to wildfire

The California Department of Finance reports Los Angeles County lost 64,000 residents between January 2025 and January 2026, attributing the loss to the Eaton and Palisades wildfires, normal flow to the Inland Empire, and deportations. The same release counts 11,160 housing units destroyed by those two fires alone. This is the single most important finding for the hold case: a shock large enough to move a county-level series is not a tail hypothetical, it is the most recent observation.

Implication: a projection model fitted to 2010–2019 growth would have been wrong in the most recent year. Any hold case must be stress-tested against a flat-to-negative demand year, not an average year.

2 · The tenant is creditworthy enough to keep paying, but it is not growing

Jack in the Box Inc.'s audited FY2025 statements show total revenues of $1.465 billion, down from $1.571bn in FY2024 and $1.692bn in FY2023 — two consecutive declines — with a net loss of $80.7 million after $209.6 million of goodwill and intangible impairment. On the other side, the leased-property side of the business is stable: franchise rental revenues were $368.6 million in FY2025 against $375.4m and $364.6m in the two prior years.

Implication for a landlord: rent collection risk on an in-place lease is low. Incentive to pay above-market rent at renewal is also low. The company discloses that roughly 70% of systemwide restaurants sit in California and Texas, so a California-only landlord and this tenant share the same geographic concentration risk.

3 · The labour market has tightened, not loosened, into 2026

Los Angeles County unemployment was 6.2% at its 2025 peak and has fallen to 4.5% in September 2026; the metro area rate is 4.4% in October 2026 on preliminary BLS figures. That is close to full employment for this county. Counter-intuitively, this is not purely good news for a restaurant landlord: leisure and hospitality added jobs at only +1.5% year-over-year while education and health services grew +3.3%, and information and financial activities shrank. Job growth is rotating toward sectors that do not generate the same daytime lunch traffic.

Implication: the demand driver to model is sector mix in the trade area, not the headline unemployment rate.

4 · The sale itself is taxable at a higher combined rate than most sellers assume

Stacking the statutory layers for a sale above the top thresholds: federal long-term capital gain 20%, unrecaptured §1250 recapture at up to 25%, net investment income tax 3.8%, California at the top printed bracket 12.30%, plus — only if the parcel is inside the City of Los Angeles — Measure ULA at 4.0% on values over $5m and 5.5% on values of $10m or more, on top of an existing 0.56% combined City and County transfer tax.

Implication: a sale is the expensive path in tax terms, so the hold case starts with a structural advantage that the assumptions must be strong enough to overcome. It is also why the after-tax module asks for your basis rather than assuming one.

IRS Topic 409 ↗ IRS Topic 559 ↗ FTB 2024 rate schedules ↗ Measure ULA ballot text ↗

5 · Hazard screening is an official lookup, not a research exercise

Three government tools answer the parcel-level hazard question definitively and cheaply: FEMA's Flood Map Service Center for the flood zone, the California Geological Survey's EQ Zapp for fault-rupture, liquefaction and landslide zones, and CAL FIRE's Fire Hazard Severity Zone viewer for fire zone status. All three are address-driven and all three were reachable during this session — which is precisely why the missing parcel address, not a lack of data, is what blocks W-04.

Note from the state's own documentation: the fire maps evaluate hazard — physical conditions over a 30–50 year horizon — not risk, the expected damage under current mitigation. Do not present a zone as a loss estimate.

The course of action this evidence supports

Not a verdict — a sequence. Each step is cheap, reversible, and closes a specific gate on the decision board. The point of the order is that the expensive, irreversible steps sit behind the cheap, information-producing ones.

  1. Identify the parcel and the jurisdiction. Nothing parcel-level can start without the address or AIN, and the jurisdiction changes the tax answer because Measure ULA is a City-of-Los-Angeles tax. Cost: minutes. Closes FLAG-U02.
  2. Pull the three hazard maps. Once the address exists, FEMA, EQ Zapp and CAL FIRE each answer in a single lookup. Cost: minutes. Closes W-04.
  3. Order the lease abstract. Exact expiry, base rent, escalations, options, expense pass-throughs, co-tenancy, and any early-termination right. Cost: an adviser's time. Closes the largest single gap, FLAG-U01.
  4. Assemble the tax file. Basis, acquisition date, accumulated depreciation, entity, filing status, material participation. Cost: a CPA hour. This is what makes the after-tax comparison real rather than illustrative.
  5. Commission comparable sales, not a forecast. Three to five closed NNN QSR or single-tenant retail trades with known lease term, so the short-lease discount is priced off quotes rather than off a model. Ties directly to FLAG-U04.
  6. Only then run the model, set the renewal ask, and choose. By this point the hold-versus-sell question has real numbers on both sides.
Why this ordering protects wealth. Steps 1–2 cost almost nothing and can kill the deal outright if a hazard zone or a jurisdiction issue appears. Step 3 changes the timing answer, which is the one thing you cannot buy back later. Step 4 changes the net proceeds answer, which is where most avoidable loss sits. Committing to a sale or a renewal before these four are closed means pricing an asset without knowing its lease, its tax basis, or its hazard exposure.

How to read this site

  • Verified — transcribed from the official page linked in the same row, with the quoted text shown.
  • Derived — arithmetic on two verified figures; the operation is stated.
  • Unverified — the cell is empty. No estimate, no proxy, no interpolation.
  • Blank is deliberate. A blank tells you which phone call to make.
  • Nothing on this site is a forecast presented as fact. Where a projection is needed, the assumptions are labelled as assumptions.
  • Evidence cut-off for every figure on this site: 2026-11-25. Each page repeats it in the header.
