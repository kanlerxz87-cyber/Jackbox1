W-02 · private worksheet extends to a sell-versus-hold comparison

Two cases, side by side — and no winner declared

The brief asked for an after-tax sale comparison alongside a negotiated renewal case, with no winner chosen until verified inputs exist. This page is that comparison, built as a calculator that runs entirely in your browser. It ships with every field empty, because a pre-filled default would be an assumption dressed as a fact.

Privacy — how this actually works. The calculator is a JavaScript file served from this page. It makes no network requests, sets no cookies, writes nothing to localStorage or sessionStorage, and has no analytics. Nothing you type is transmitted to any server, including this one. Close the tab and the numbers are gone. You can confirm this by opening your browser's network panel while using it — it will show no requests beyond the page assets themselves. The stacking order, as the calculator applies it

  1. Net sale proceeds = gross price − selling costs − Measure ULA and transfer taxes if applicable.
  2. Adjusted basis = original basis − accumulated depreciation.
  3. Realised gain = net proceeds − adjusted basis. If negative, no tax and no recapture.
  4. Split the gain into unrecaptured §1250 gain (up to the depreciation taken) and long-term capital gain (the rest).
  5. Federal: §1250 portion at 25%; capital portion at 15% or 20% depending on total taxable income.
  6. Net investment income tax: 3.8% on the lesser of net investment income or MAGI above the threshold — applied here to the gain, conditional on passive treatment.
  7. California: the whole gain at the top printed marginal bracket, since capital gain is ordinary income in California.
  8. Cash in hand = net proceeds − all tax − debt repaid.

Each line in the calculator's output corresponds to one of these steps, so the arithmetic can be checked against the source rate rather than trusted.

Three things the calculator deliberately does not do.
  1. It does not apply Measure ULA automatically. ULA is a City-of-Los-Angeles tax; if the parcel sits in an adjacent incorporated city it does not apply at all, and applying it wrongly would overstate the tax by 4–5.5% of the entire sale price. Add it manually once FLAG-U02 is closed.
  2. It does not model a §1031 exchange. A 1031 defers the federal gain entirely if replacement property is identified and acquired on time, which can dominate every other variable on this page. That is an adviser conversation, not a formula.
  3. It does not model the 1% California Mental Health Services Tax above $1,000,000 of taxable income, because that is a separate statute from the Form 540 schedules cited here (FLAG-U09).

The negotiated-renewal case — the third number

The brief pairs the sale case with a negotiated renewal, not just a passive hold. That is the right frame, because a renewal changes the exit capitalisation rate itself: a buyer pays less for a property with two years of term than for the same property with ten. The renewal case is therefore not "hold at whatever rent" — it is a trade:

Why the tenant's own disclosures matter to the renewal ask

Jack in the Box discloses a portfolio weighted-average operating lease discount rate of 6.1% and a weighted-average remaining operating lease term of 11.2 years. Those two numbers are the tenant's own view of the cost and duration of its real estate. A renewal ask that prices the landlord's capital at well above 6.1% is asking the tenant to accept a worse deal than its own book — it may simply not renew. A renewal ask that prices it well below is leaving money on the table. The defensible zone sits around the tenant's disclosed cost of lease capital, adjusted for the specific store's sales.

What is still missing to turn that into a rent number: the store's sales, because Jack in the Box's franchise rent is structured on a percentage of sales as well as a base. Without store-level sales, a percentage-rent clause cannot be valued.

What to do with the output

  1. Write down which input dominates. Run the calculator, then change one field at a time. In almost every version of this model, the exit cap rate and the hold horizon dominate everything else. That tells you where to spend adviser money.
  2. Send the same inputs to a CPA. The point of the calculator is to make the conversation specific — "here is my basis, here is my horizon, here is my cap assumption, what is my net" beats "should I sell?".
  3. Do not treat the sensitivity table as a probability distribution. It is a one-variable-at-a-time stress, not a Monte Carlo. Correlated moves — a cap-rate expansion alongside a NOI decline, which is what a real downturn looks like — are worse than any single row shows. The risk register covers that case.
  4. Revisit when the lease abstract lands. Once the exact expiry and escalator are known, the hold horizon stops being a guess and the comparison becomes decision-grade.
