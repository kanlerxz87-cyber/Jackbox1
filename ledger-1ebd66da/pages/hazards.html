W-04

Parcel-level hazard screening

Regional hazard context is close to worthless for a single parcel: a "high wildfire risk in Southern California" headline tells you nothing about whether this building is in a mapped zone. Three government lookups answer the question definitively, by address, in minutes. This page documents them, states exactly what each produces, and stress-tests the three ways a hazard actually reaches the owner's balance sheet.

Status: blocked on one input — the address. Every row in the parcel table below is empty because the parcel has not been identified. This is the single highest-value gap in the entire workspace relative to the cost of closing it: three lookups, no fee, no adviser required. FLAG-U02 →

The three official lookups

What each authority maps, and what the output means
HazardOfficial sourceWhat it returns for a parcelWhat it does not mean
Flood FEMA Flood Map Service Center ↗ The effective Flood Insurance Rate Map zone for the address, and the base flood elevation where published. Not a flood loss estimate. A zone outside the 1%-annual-chance boundary can still flood, and the map is periodically revised.
Seismic California Geological Survey — EQ Zapp ↗ Whether the parcel lies in a mapped zone for fault rupture, liquefaction, or earthquake-induced landslide — and whether CGS has evaluated the area at all. Not a shaking-intensity or damage estimate. The state is explicit that mapped zones may include land where hazards were already mitigated to local standards.
Wildfire CAL FIRE / Office of the State Fire Marshal — FHSZ ↗ Moderate / High / Very High zone for State Responsibility Area, and the recommended Local Responsibility Area zone from the 2025 rollout. Not a risk score. CAL FIRE states that the maps evaluate hazard — physical conditions over a 30–50 year period — not risk, which accounts for mitigation.

Two distinctions the state itself draws — and they matter at a closing table

Hazard vs risk. CAL FIRE's own documentation: "Fire Hazard Severity Zone maps evaluate 'hazard', not 'risk' … 'Hazard' is based on the physical conditions that create a likelihood and expected fire behavior over a 30 to 50-year period without considering mitigation measures … 'Risk' is the potential damage a fire can do to the area under existing conditions."

A buyer who is told "Very High hazard" may hear "high risk". They are not the same, and a seller with documented mitigation can argue the gap.

Already-mitigated land. CGS notes that "Seismic Hazard Zones identified on the map may include developed land where delineated hazards have already been mitigated to city or county standards" and directs the reader to the local building department.

For an operating restaurant built decades ago, this is the most likely outcome: a mapped zone on a site that already has engineered mitigation. The distinction is worth an hour of a geotechnical engineer's time before it is conceded to a buyer.

How a hazard actually reaches the owner

The brief asked to "stress loss of rent, repairs and exit liquidity together". Those are three separate transmission channels and they behave very differently — which is why bundling them into a single "hazard multiplier" is a mistake.

Three channels, three mechanisms No probability is assigned to any scenario below. Assigning one without an official source would breach the no-inference rule; the insurer and the engineer are the sources for those numbers.
ChannelMechanismWho bears it under a typical NNN leaseWhat would quantify it
Loss of rent The building is unusable for a period. The tenant may abate rent, or may have a termination right after a stated period of untenantability. Often the landlord, unless business-interruption coverage assigned to the lease backfills it. Abatement and termination language is usually in the lease. The lease's casualty clause (FLAG-U01) plus a rent-loss insurance quote for the specific address.
Repairs and rebuild Physical damage, plus the gap between insured replacement cost and the cost to rebuild to current code. The landlord, subject to insurance proceeds and any deductible. Code-upgrade cost is frequently excluded. A current replacement-cost appraisal and a property policy declarations page, both addressed to the parcel.
Exit liquidity The buyer pool narrows. Lenders reprice or withdraw. A property in a mapped zone can become temporarily unsaleable regardless of rent. The seller, at the worst possible moment — liquidity risk is highest when a shock has just occurred. Comparable post-event transaction evidence. None is collected here; this is the honest limit of the analysis.
Why these three must be stressed together, not separately. They correlate. A fire that closes the store also concentrates the local buyer pool, raises construction costs region-wide, and pushes insurers to withdraw — all in the same month. A model that shocks rent by −10% and leaves the exit cap rate alone will understate the damage, because the two move in the same direction at the same time. Any stress test worth running puts all three in the same scenario.

The countervailing evidence — and its limit

Hazard exposure is not uniform across a county of 9.8 million

The same DOF release that reports the population loss records 11,160 housing units lost to the Eaton and Palisades fires — and also records that Los Angeles County added 11,260 net housing units in the same year. Both are true at once because the fires were geographically concentrated while construction was not.

What this proves: a county-scale disaster can be devastating locally and roughly neutral county-wide in aggregate. It therefore proves nothing about this parcel — which is exactly the argument for doing the parcel-level lookup rather than reading the county headline.

Insurance, engineering and the questions to ask

Insurance questions

  • What is the current policy's replacement-cost basis, and when was it last appraised? Under-insurance is the most common silent gap.
  • Is business-interruption / rent-loss coverage assigned to the landlord or the tenant?
  • Is flood coverage separate, and is the parcel in a zone that makes it mandatory for a federally backed loan?
  • Is earthquake coverage carried at all? In California it is usually excluded from a standard property policy and priced separately.
  • Has any carrier issued a non-renewal or a surcharge in the last three policy years? In the current California market this is often the first signal of a reassessed hazard.

Engineering questions

  • Is there a geotechnical report on file, and does it address liquefaction for this specific site?
  • If EQ Zapp shows a mapped zone, was the hazard mitigated at construction, and is there a record with the local building department?
  • What is the structural system and its expected performance in a significant shaking event?
  • Is the building sprinklered, and what would a rebuild to current code cost versus the insured value?
  • What access exists for emergency response, and does the site configuration create a defensible-space constraint?
Honest limitation. This page could not screen the parcel because the parcel was not identified. It therefore publishes the method, the official tools and the stress frame — and refuses to substitute a regional proxy, because a regional hazard statement applied to a specific parcel is precisely the kind of inference that produces a wrong decision with high confidence.
