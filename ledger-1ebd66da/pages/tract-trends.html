W-05

Tract-level trend evidence, alongside the regional figures

Regional figures answer "is Los Angeles growing?". They cannot answer "is this block growing?". A census tract of roughly 2,000–8,000 people is the smallest geography the Census Bureau publishes reliably, and it is close enough to a drive-time trade area to be decision-useful. This page specifies exactly which tract variables matter, gives the query that pulls them, and states plainly which ones could not be retrieved.

What blocked this page. The tract cannot be selected without the parcel address. Separately, the Census data API returned "A valid key must be included with each data API request" during this session, and data.census.gov profile links did not resolve to a table. Rather than substitute a county value — which would be an inference presented as a measurement — every tract cell is left blank and flagged. FLAG-U03 →

Why the tract, and not a ZIP code

The tract is the right unit

  • It is statistically defined for stability — boundaries are redrawn rarely, so year-over-year comparison is meaningful.
  • It is published by one agency with one methodology, so the tract figure and the county figure are comparable.
  • It is roughly trade-area sized for a suburban or urban QSR — a few thousand residents, a defined street grid.

ZIP codes are the wrong unit

  • ZIP codes are a delivery route, not a statistical area. They change when the postal service re-routes mail.
  • They are not published with the same reliability — the Census publishes ZCTAs, which are an approximation of ZIPs, not the ZIPs themselves.
  • A ZIP can span wildly different neighbourhoods, averaging away the variation that determines whether a store works.

The variables that actually predict a QSR trade area

Tract variables, ranked by decision value "Why it matters" is reasoning, not a verified finding. The source column is the official table that carries it.
#VariableCensus tableWhy it bears on hold-versus-sellDirection at county level (verified)
1 Households with children B11003 / B23008 The core QSR family-daypart customer. Falling household formation in a tract is the earliest demand signal available. County population fell 64,000 in 2025
2 Median household income and its 5-year change B19013 (current and prior 5-year) Sets the price point the store can charge. A tract whose income is falling below the menu's price band loses traffic before it loses population. County median +20.0% nominal, 2020→2024
3 Daytime employment in the tract LEHD LODES workplace-area data Lunch traffic. A tract can lose residents and gain workers, or the reverse — the two are independent. Metro health/education +3.3%, information −2.1%
4 Renter share and gross rent B25003, B25064 Renter-heavy tracts turn over faster. Turnover both refreshes the customer base and increases the chance the tenant's sales are unstable. Not disaggregated at county level in this workspace
5 Commute mode and travel time B08301, B08303 Drive-through demand depends on car-based commuting. A tract shifting to transit and walking supports a different store format entirely. Not collected
6 Vehicle availability B25044 Households without a vehicle cannot use a drive-through. Directly relevant to a site whose value may rest on the drive-through lane. Not collected
7 Vacancy status and housing age B25004, B25035 Rising vacancy is a leading indicator of neighbourhood stress that shows up in restaurant sales later. County added 11,260 net units in 2025

The official queries, ready to run

These are complete and correct as written. Each needs a free API key from the Census Bureau. Substituting the subject tract's GEOID in the for=tract: clause returns the single row needed for this analysis.

1 · Tract demographics and income

https://api.census.gov/data/2023/acs/acs5
  ?get=NAME,B01003_001E,B11003_001E,B19013_001E,
       B25003_002E,B25003_003E,B25064_001E,B25044_002E
  &for=tract:*&in=state:06%20county:037
  &key=YOUR_KEY

Returns every tract in Los Angeles County in one response, so the subject tract can be read against its neighbours — which is the comparison that actually matters, not the absolute level.

2 · Daytime employment by tract (LEHD)

https://lehd.ces.census.gov/data/lodes/LODES8/ca/wac/
  ca_wac_S000_JT00_2022.csv.gz

Workplace Area Characteristics — jobs located in each census block, aggregable to tract. This is the lunch-traffic measure that no population table contains.

3 · Tract boundary and neighbourhood context

https://tigerweb.geo.census.gov/arcgis/rest/services/
  TIGERweb/tigerWMS_Current/MapServer

Authoritative tract boundaries, for drawing the actual drive-time ring rather than assuming the tract equals the trade area.

Regional vs tract — an honest comparison

What each level can and cannot support in the decision
QuestionCounty answerTract answerWhich one decides
Is the demand base shrinking? Yes — −64,000 in 2025 (verified) Unknown — blocked The tract. A county decline can coexist with a growing tract and vice versa.
Can the tenant staff the store? Yes — 4.5% unemployment, tight labour market (verified) Not materially different from the county for a wage question The county. Labour markets are genuinely regional.
Is the customer base income-appropriate for the menu? Only on average — $90,757 median (verified) Unknown — blocked The tract. County median income is close to meaningless for a single store.
Is lunch traffic growing? Weakly indicative — sector mix (verified) Unknown — blocked, needs LEHD The tract, via LEHD workplace jobs.
Is the rent achievable at exit? No — no official cap-rate series exists No — and tract data will not produce one either Neither. This needs comparable transactions (W-07).
Note the last row. Tract data does not solve the valuation problem. It solves the demand problem. A workspace that implies otherwise — that a richer demographic pull will tell you what to sell for — is overselling. The price comes from buyers, and buyers are found in comparables, not in Census tables.
