Review section

Suggestions — pick a path before implementing anything

This section exists so a reviewer can choose. Every suggestion is scored on four dimensions, states what it would change, and names its dependency. Nothing here has been implemented beyond what is already live, and each item is deliberately small enough to accept or reject on its own merits.

How to use this page. Read the score, then read the "what it changes" column. Anything scoring 4+ on impact with 2 or less on effort is worth doing immediately. Anything with a dependency stays parked until that dependency clears — doing it early would produce a confident answer built on nothing.

Recommended path — if a reviewer wants a single answer

Do S-01, then S-02 and S-03 in the same sitting

Three actions, one afternoon, no fee. Together they close four of the six decision gates and convert the largest block of blank cells on this site into verified findings. Nothing else on this page produces as much decision value per hour of effort.

Why not start with the lease abstract (S-06)? It has higher impact but it requires an adviser engagement, and its usefulness depends on knowing which jurisdiction and which hazard posture you are working with. If the parcel turns out to be in an adjacent city rather than the City of Los Angeles, the tax analysis changes, and if it sits in a mapped hazard zone the renewal strategy changes. Do the free work first, then commission the paid work with better targeting.

What would change this recommendation: if the owner already knows the jurisdiction and the hazard posture, S-06 becomes the first move and should start immediately, in parallel with everything in Tier 1.

What a reviewer should reject

Improvements deliberately not made — and why
Not doingReason
Publishing an estimated cap rateNo official source exists. A plausible number would be the most damaging possible addition to this site because it would be quoted without its caveat.
Publishing an estimated parcel addressThe brief did not supply one. Guessing an address would attach verified hazard and zoning data to the wrong property — worse than publishing nothing.
Filling unverified cells with a "typical" valueA typical value for someone else's lease is not information about this lease. It would convert an honest blank into a confident error.
Adding a headline recommendationThe brief explicitly asked for no winner until verified inputs exist. A confident recommendation built on six blocked gates would be the exact failure the brief guards against.
Adding analytics or trackingContradicts the privacy requirement. The site has no scripts beyond its own two files.
Adding an email capture formCollecting contact data would create the personal-information exposure this workspace is built to avoid.
