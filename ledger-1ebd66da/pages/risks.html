Risk

Risk register — every path, every failure mode

The brief asked for the risks of every possible decision, business-as-usual and black swan, weighed against the preservation of wealth and the opportunity for growth. This register does that without inventing probabilities: it rates likelihood and impact qualitatively, names the mitigation, and states which decision each risk actually changes.

On the absence of numbers. A risk register with invented probabilities is worse than one without any, because the numbers get quoted and then treated as measured. Nothing below carries a percentage that is not sourced. Where an official observation exists — a peak unemployment rate, a population change, a fire's housing loss — it is quoted and linked, because that is evidence about the scale of a shock, not a forecast of its recurrence.

How to read the ratings

Likelihood

  • Near-certain — already happening or scheduled
  • Likely — expected within a normal hold period
  • Possible — plausible, not expected
  • Rare — requires an unusual event
  • Tail — low frequency, severe consequence

Impact

  • Severe — capital loss above a normal market cycle
  • High — materially changes net proceeds or optionality
  • Moderate — affects timing or a single year's cash flow
  • Low — administrative or recoverable

Scope

  • A — only if you sell
  • B — only if you renew
  • C — only if you hold to expiry
  • All — affects every path

Path A — risks of selling

Selling risks
IDRiskLikelihoodImpactWhy it bitesMitigation
R-01 Selling into the short-lease discount Near-certainHigh The buyer prices remaining term. As expiry approaches, the cap rate widens and the buyer pool shrinks. This is the default outcome of selling late. Either renew before marketing, or accept and price the discount explicitly using W-07's comparable evidence.
R-02 Tax friction exceeding expectation LikelyHigh Stacked federal 20% + §1250 recapture 25% + NIIT 3.8% + California 12.30%, plus up to 5.5% ULA inside the City of LA. On a low-basis asset the blended effective rate can exceed 35%. Get the CPA projection before listing. Evaluate a §1031 exchange, an instalment sale, or a charitable remainder structure. These are adviser conversations, not formulas.
R-03 Measure ULA applied in error, or missed PossibleHigh ULA is 4.0% of the entire price above $5m and 5.5% above $10m, inside the City of LA only. If the parcel is in an adjacent city it does not apply at all; if it does apply and is missed, the seller absorbs it at closing. Confirm jurisdiction from the assessor record before pricing (FLAG-U02). Have escrow calculate ULA explicitly and show it on the settlement statement.
R-04 Buyer financing failure after contract RareHigh A short lease already limits lender appetite. If rates move against the buyer mid-escrow, the loan can fail — and time has been lost inside the window that matters most. Weight all-cash and low-leverage bidders. Keep a backup offer. Price for a fast close.
R-05 Due-diligence discovery LikelyHigh A hazard-zone designation, an unreconciled CAM history, an unfunded landlord obligation, or a lease discrepancy found by the buyer's counsel becomes a price reduction or a walk-away. Do the same diligence the buyer will do, first. That is what G2 and G3 are for. Discovering your own problem is cheap; being told about it is expensive.
R-06 Capital gains rate change during the marketing period PossibleModerate Statutory rates are set by Congress and the Legislature. A rate change between listing and closing changes net proceeds without changing the asset. Track the legislative calendar. Where a change is scheduled, closing before the effective date can be worth more than a small price concession.

Path B — risks of renewing

Renewal risks
IDRiskLikelihoodImpactWhy it bitesMitigation
R-07 Locking in below-market rent for a long term PossibleHigh A long extension at a concessionary rent converts a temporary soft patch into a decade of underperformance, and the loss is crystallised at the next sale. Shorter extension with a second option, or a rent reset at a stated year. Never trade a long term for a discount without a mid-term reset.
R-08 Tenant credit deterioration during the new term Near-certain to be testedHigh The tenant reported a net loss of $80.7m in FY2025 and $36.7m in FY2024, with $209.6m and $162.6m of impairment. Revenue fell two years running. A lease is only as good as the payer. Corporate guaranty rather than franchisee-only. Letter of credit or security deposit. Monitor the 10-K annually — it is public.
R-09 Granting away future upside via cheap options LikelyHigh If the existing option rent is fixed and low, the landlord has already lost the renewal upside and may not know it until the option is exercised. Abstract the option rent mechanism first (A4). If it is below market, negotiate a reset as the price of any further concession.
R-10 Sunk capital into a store that later closes PossibleModerate TI or a rent holiday given in exchange for term is lost if the tenant closes the store, unless the lease has recapture or clawback language. Amortise any contribution over the term with clawback on early termination. Never fund TI unconditionally.

Path C — risks of holding to expiry

Hold-to-expiry risks The highest-variance path: it keeps the upside and keeps all the downside.
IDRiskLikelihoodImpactWhy it bitesMitigation
R-11 Vacancy and prolonged downtime at expiry PossibleSevere A purpose-built QSR box can sit empty for well over a year. Rent stops, property tax and insurance continue, and the asset is unsaleable while vacant. Pre-marketing to replacement tenants during the final lease year. Model a zero-income year explicitly in the hold case.
R-12 Re-leasing cost exceeding expectation PossibleHigh TI, leasing commissions, and a rent-free period on a specialised building can consume years of rent. Get a broker's re-leasing budget before choosing this path, not after.
R-13 Zoning or use restriction limiting re-letting PossibleSevere If the drive-through is a non-conforming use, a new tenant may not be able to operate one — which can cut the site's value to land value. Confirm zoning and non-conforming-use status with the city planning department. This is a pre-condition to choosing path C.
R-14 Redevelopment opportunity never materialises RareSevere The hold case often rests on an implicit land-value option. If entitlement, financing or demand does not appear, the owner has held a depreciating building for nothing. Test the land-value story against a specific entitlement path and a specific buyer, not a hope. If neither can be named, it is not an option.
R-15 Capitalisation-rate expansion RareHigh With the 10-year Treasury at 4.92%, a rise in the risk-free rate compresses values regardless of the lease. This is macro, not property-specific, and it cannot be mitigated by good management. Stress the exit cap upward. Note that this risk is reduced by selling, which is the strongest argument for path A in a rising-rate environment.

Tail risks — shocks that can destroy local value

These are the "black swan" scenarios the brief asked for. Each is rated on frequency and consequence, and each is grounded in something that has actually happened in this county rather than imagined.

R-16 · Wildfire reaching an urban parcel

Observed basis: the Eaton and Palisades fires destroyed 11,160 housing units in Los Angeles County in 2025, and the state attributes part of the county's 64,000-person population loss to them. This is not a hypothetical — it is the most recent year in the data.

Why it matters even for an urban parcel: the direct burn risk to a central-LA commercial site is low. The indirect risk is not: regional construction cost inflation, insurance withdrawal and repricing, buyer-pool contraction, and the county-level demand shock all follow a major fire regardless of where the flames were.

Mitigation: parcel-level FHSZ lookup (W-04); insurance review for non-renewal risk; and a hold-case stress that includes a demand decline and a cap-rate widening in the same year.

R-17 · Earthquake

Observed basis: Southern California is seismically active and the state maintains official hazard-zone mapping for fault rupture, liquefaction and landslide precisely because the ground-failure risk is real and non-uniform. The CGS notes that a single event "will not uniformly affect the entire area zoned".

Why it is a wealth risk specifically: earthquake coverage is typically excluded from a standard commercial property policy and priced separately, so an uninsured or underinsured owner faces an unbounded repair liability on an asset that may also be their largest single holding. The loss-of-rent and exit-liquidity channels arrive at the same time.

Mitigation: EQ Zapp lookup; a current replacement-cost appraisal; a separate earthquake quote; and a geotechnical review of any mapped zone to determine whether the hazard was already mitigated at construction.

R-18 · Insurance market failure

Why it is on this list and usually is not on others: a hazard the owner can insure is a managed risk. A hazard the owner cannot insure is a title problem — buyers cannot finance an uninsurable asset, so the parcel becomes illiquid at any price. California's property insurance market has been repricing, and non-renewal notices are the earliest observable signal.

Mitigation: ask the current carrier directly about renewal appetite. Talk to a broker about alternatives before listing. Treat a non-renewal notice as a decision trigger, not an administrative task.

R-19 · Tenant insolvency or mass closure

Observed basis: the tenant's own FY2025 filing discloses a net loss, two consecutive years of revenue decline, and substantial impairment charges. It also discloses concentration risk: approximately 70% of systemwide restaurants are in California and Texas, so a California-specific shock hits the tenant and the landlord together.

Why it is not "unlikely": a credit tenant's covenant is the foundation of a single-tenant NNN value. Even a profitable-chain closure of one store is a full vacancy for the landlord. In bankruptcy, lease rejection can extinguish the remaining term.

Mitigation: read the 10-K annually — it is public and free. Require a corporate guaranty. Maintain a reserve sized to one vacancy year. Do not treat "NNN" as a substitute for credit analysis.

R-20 · Interest-rate regime change

Observed basis: the 10-year Treasury traded down to 3.97% on 2026-02-27, then rose to 4.92% on 2026-11-23, trading above 5.20% in late September and October 2026 (5.24% on 2026-09-28; 5.31% on 2026-10-05). A cap rate is, in part, a spread over that rate.

Why it is near-certain to be tested: every hold case carries an exit assumption. If rates are 100bp higher at exit, the exit value falls by a large fraction of the asset's value, independent of anything the owner does. This is the risk that most cleanly favours selling over holding — not because the property is weak, but because the discount rate is outside the owner's control.

Mitigation: none available to an unlevered owner other than timing. A levered owner can fix the rate. Either way, the sensitivity table in the worksheet shows the exposure directly.

Weighing wealth preservation against growth

The trade-off, stated without a recommendation

The brief asked for a balance between preserving wealth and keeping growth opportunity, at a manageable level of risk. The evidence supports a specific framing of that trade-off, though not a specific choice:

What preserving wealth argues for

  • Selling converts a concentrated, single-asset, single-tenant position into diversified capital.
  • Selling before term decays avoids the near-certain short-lease discount (R-01).
  • Selling removes the uninsured-hazard exposure (R-16, R-17, R-18) from the balance sheet.
  • At a 4.92% risk-free rate, the opportunity cost of holding a low-yield real asset is measurable and currently high.

What keeping growth opportunity argues for

  • A sale is taxed at a combined rate that can exceed 35% on a low-basis asset — a permanent, not a deferrable, loss unless a 1031 is used.
  • Real estate is the only asset here that can be financed; selling converts financeable capital into taxed cash.
  • Land value in central Los Angeles has an option value that a leased-fee sale does not capture.
  • Renewing first and selling after captures both the term premium and the tax deferral.

The synthesis the evidence supports: the "renew first, then sell" path is the only one that appears on both lists — it preserves the tax deferral and the financing capacity while removing the short-lease discount. It is also the path that requires the most preparation, which is why it is not the default. Whether it dominates depends entirely on the size of the term coefficient in W-07 and the renewal rent, both of which are currently unknown.

This is a description of the trade-off, not a recommendation. The recommendation cannot be made until G3 and G4 clear.

One asymmetry worth internalising. The downside of selling at a slightly low price is a one-time, bounded loss. The downside of holding a single-tenant asset through a tenant default, a hazard event, or an insurance withdrawal is unbounded and correlated with the owner's other local exposure. For an owner whose wealth is concentrated in this asset, that asymmetry argues for weighting the sale case more heavily than its expected-value arithmetic alone would suggest.
