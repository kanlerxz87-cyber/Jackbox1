W-01

Trade-area economics — replacing the city/county proxy

A restaurant's trade area is a drive-time ring, not a county line. But a county line is what the official statistical series measure, and this page refuses to invent a smaller number than the one that actually exists. So it does the next honest thing: it publishes the tightest verified geographies available, states exactly what each one can and cannot tell you, and hands over the official query that produces the tract-level figure once the parcel address exists.

On this page

  1. The granularity problem, stated plainly
  2. Demand: population and households
  3. Income
  4. Labour market
  5. Sector mix — the traffic driver
  6. Prices and rates
  7. The 2025 shock and why it changes the model
  8. What these numbers do and do not support

1 · The granularity problem, stated plainly

Three geographies are in play, and they are not interchangeable:

What each geography can answer
GeographyWhat it tells youWhat it cannot tell youStatus
Los Angeles County
~9.8m people		 Whether the demand base is growing or shrinking; the labour market the tenant recruits from; the price level that drives menu pricing. Anything about the block. A county of 9.8 million contains both the strongest and weakest trade areas in the region. Available
LA–Long Beach–Santa Ana MSA Sector-level job counts as a 12-month direction; the CPI area that governs any CPI-linked escalation clause. Sub-metro variation. Note the CPI area is LA–Long Beach–Anaheim, a different geography again. Available
Census tract (the real trade area) Tract population, median household income, renter share, commute mode, household size — the variables that actually predict lunch traffic. Requires an address to select the tract. Could not be retrieved in this session. Blocked
FLAG-U03 — the tract cannot be selected without the parcel address. The Census data API requires a key; without one it returned "A valid key must be included" during this session, and data.census.gov deep links did not resolve to a table. Nothing has been substituted. The exact official query is published below so the figure can be pulled in one step once the address exists. See the tract-trends page →

The official query, ready to run — this returns total population, median household income and median gross rent for every tract in the county, so the subject tract can be read in one pass:

https://api.census.gov/data/2023/acs/acs5
  ?get=NAME,B01003_001E,B19013_001E,B25064_001E,B25003_002E,B25003_003E
  &for=tract:*&in=state:06%20county:037
  &key=YOUR_KEY

Source: Census Bureau — ACS Data via API ↗. A free key is issued at api.census.gov/data/key_signup.html ↗.

The 2025 → 2026 move is unusually large. County unemployment went from a 6.2% peak in mid-2025 to 4.5% by September 2026. For context, the same series peaked at 18.9% in May 2020 and 13.1% in July 2010. The 2025 episode was therefore far milder than either — but it is the one that coincides with the population loss, which is a combination with no clean precedent in this series.

5 · Sector mix — the traffic driver

Headline employment hides a rotation. Education and health services are growing at more than double the rate of leisure and hospitality, while information, manufacturing and financial activities are shrinking in 12-month terms. Health and education generate campus and hospital traffic with predictable shift patterns; information and finance generate office lunch traffic. These are different trade areas even within one submarket.

One escalation consequence worth flagging now. If the lease escalates on CPI-W (currently +4.1% 12-month) rather than a fixed step, then the landlord's rent is currently growing faster than CPI-U (+3.7%) — favourable during the term. The reverse is also true in a disinflation: a CPI-linked clause protects the landlord less than a fixed escalator when prices fall. Which clause applies is unknown, which is why it is FLAG-U01.

7 · The 2025 shock, and why it changes the model

A county-level series moved because of a fire

The California Department of Finance explicitly attributes part of Los Angeles County's 64,000-person loss to the Eaton and Palisades wildfires, alongside normal Inland-Empire outflow and deportations. The same release records 11,160 housing units destroyed by those two fires and a statewide net housing change that nets out to +115,165 units despite an 11,406-unit fire loss.

Why this belongs on the economics page and not only the hazard page: it is direct evidence that a physical shock in this county is large enough to change a demographic series within one year. A hold case that models demand as a smooth trend line is mis-specified. The model has to be able to produce a negative year, because the most recent year was one.

8 · What these numbers do and do not support

Supported

  • The regional demand base shrank in the most recent year, for the first time in four.
  • Household income is rising in nominal terms, faster than the 2020 baseline but only modestly ahead of local CPI.
  • The labour market is tight, so the tenant can staff the store and wage pressure on franchisee margins is real.
  • Job growth is rotating from office- and goods-based sectors toward health and education.
  • Long-term rates near 4.92% set a high hurdle for any leveraged hold case.

Not supported — do not cite this page for these

  • Any statement about the parcel's own trade area. County figures cannot be shrunk.
  • Any cap rate. No official series publishes submarket NNN retail cap rates (FLAG-U04).
  • Any projection beyond the next observation. Nothing here is a forecast.
  • Any conclusion about school quality. This workspace contains no school-performance evidence, so the school factor is an open question rather than a finding (see Q-11).
  • Any comparison against a specific competitor. Competitor evidence was not collected.
Deliberate omission, logged as a gap. The brief asked for school systems and competitor evidence. Neither was collected, and inventing a ranking from memory would breach the no-hallucination rule. Both are carried as open questions with a named source that would close them, rather than as weak findings.
