FLAG-U07 The brief's action verbs imply mutually exclusive conclusions The brief asks simultaneously to "determine the best course of action" and to "not select a winner until verified inputs exist". Resolved in favour of the second instruction, because it is the more specific and the more defensible. This workspace therefore publishes a sequence of actions and a decision framework, not a verdict. Recorded here so the choice is visible and reversible. —
FLAG-U08 "Around central Los Angeles" is not a jurisdiction The phrase could describe the City of Los Angeles, but also Vernon, Huntington Park, Maywood, Bell, Cudahy, Commerce, or unincorporated County territory — all adjacent to central LA. Each has different transfer taxes, rent rules and planning regimes. Treated as an input requirement rather than an assumption. LA County Assessor Portal ↗
FLAG-U09 California Mental Health Services Tax not modelled The 1% surcharge on taxable income above $1,000,000 is a separate statute from the Form 540 rate schedules cited here. The after-tax calculator uses the 12.30% top printed bracket and therefore understates California tax on a large gain. Flagged rather than estimated, because the surcharge interacts with the base in a way that needs the statute text and a CPA. FTB 2024 rate schedules ↗ plus the separate statute — to be obtained and verified before the figure is added.

Contradictions inside the brief, and how each was resolved

Three tensions, resolved explicitly Recorded so a reviewer can disagree with the resolution rather than having to discover it.
#TensionSide ASide BResolution adopted
1 Decide, versus don't decide yet "I want to know what the best course of action and decision to make is." "Prepare an after-tax sale comparison … without selecting a winner until verified inputs exist." Side B. It is the more specific instruction, and it is the only one compatible with the no-hallucination rule. The workspace delivers the full framework, the sequence of actions, and the exact price of closing each gap.
2 Deep research, versus no inference "Do deep research to build a reliable projection system for the area." "Never infer or fill in values. If a value can't be confirmed from an official source, leave it blank." Side B governs the data; Side A governs the method. The projection method is specified in full (W-07's regression, the stress frame, the discounting approach). No projection number is published, because none can be verified.
3 Public site, versus private data "Create a github page live site … containing all entries and findings." "Personal information … must not appear in the final website product." Both, separated by construction. Everything aggregate and non-identifying is published. Everything identifying — parcel, lease, owner, tax facts — is excluded and its absence is visible as a blank with a flag. The calculator holds private inputs only inside the reader's browser.

What is deliberately absent from this site

Excluded by design — privacy

  • Parcel address, AIN, or legal description
  • Owner name, entity name, or any identifying detail
  • Any lease term, rent figure, or date from the actual lease
  • Tenant contact details or store-level sales
  • Any personal financial figure, tax return data, or guarantor information
  • Any photograph or document scan of the property

The verification script enforces this: the build fails if a street address, coordinate pair, phone number, email address, or identifier pattern appears in any published page.

Excluded by design — no verified source

  • Any capitalisation rate for the submarket
  • Any estimate of the property's value
  • Any projection of future rent or sales
  • School performance rankings
  • Competitor density or traffic counts
  • Any probability assigned to a risk scenario

Each of these is listed on the suggestions page as a candidate improvement with the source that would be required to add it properly.

How to submit a correction

Every claim on this site carries an ID — a metric row (M-xxx), a flag (FLAG-Uxx), a question (Q-xx), a risk (R-xx) or a suggestion (S-xx). A correction is most useful when it names the ID, states what is wrong, and links the official source that supersedes it.

A correction that cannot cite an official source will be added to this page as an open item rather than applied to the data, because the rule that governs the dataset is that nothing enters it unverified. That applies to corrections as much as to the original figures.
