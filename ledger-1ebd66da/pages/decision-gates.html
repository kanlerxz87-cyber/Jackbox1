W-03

Decision-gate progress board

A gate is a binary test, not a score. It is clear only when the named evidence is in hand, partial when some of it is, and blocked when the evidence does not exist yet. Gates are ordered: later gates inherit the outputs of earlier ones, so working out of order wastes money.

1 of 6 gates clear · 2 partial · 3 blocked — as of the evidence cut-off
The sale-versus-renewal commitment sits behind G3 and G4. Neither can be cleared with the information provided. That is not a research failure — it is the finding. G3 needs a lease document and G4 needs the owner's tax file, and neither exists in the public record.

Blocked by: the brief gives a range (2030–2035), which cannot be modelled as a trigger, and no rent schedule was provided. Owner of the evidence: a private lease abstract commissioned from counsel or a lease-administration firm — not a public source. W-08 → · FLAG-U01

Blocked by: adjusted basis, acquisition date, accumulated depreciation, entity structure and material participation are all unknown. The statutory rates are verified; the owner's facts are not. Owner of the evidence: the owner's CPA. W-02 / W-09 → · FLAG-U01, FLAG-U05

Held: a verified risk-free reference rate (10-year Treasury 4.92% on 2026-11-23) and the tenant's own disclosed lease discount rate (6.1%) — both useful anchors, neither a cap rate. Still missing: three to five closed NNN QSR or single-tenant retail trades with known remaining term. No official statistical series publishes submarket NNN cap rates. W-07 → · FLAG-U04

Held: county and metro series, verified and current. Still missing: the Census tract cannot be selected without the address, and the Census API requires a key. The exact query is published ready to run. W-05 → · FLAG-U03

Sequencing rule

Cheapest irreversible step last

The gates are ordered by the ratio of information gained to cost and irreversibility. G1 cost nothing and is done. G2 costs a few lookups. G3 costs an adviser's time but produces the single largest information gain. G4 costs a CPA hour. G5 costs a broker's comps. G6 costs a data pull.

Every one of those is reversible — you can walk away from all six having spent only fees. The first irreversible step is signing a renewal or accepting a purchase and sale agreement. Putting that behind G1–G6 is the whole point of the board.

A gate that stays blocked for more than one review cycle should be escalated as a decision, not left open: either the evidence is obtainable and someone must be engaged to get it, or the decision has to be taken under uncertainty with the uncertainty priced in. Leaving it open is the worst of the three, because it costs optionality without buying information.
