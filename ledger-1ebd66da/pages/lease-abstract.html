W-08

Private lease abstract, and its reconciliation with the notice calendar

A commercial lease abstract belongs in an adviser's file, never on a public page. What can live here is the structure: which fields the abstract must capture, why each one changes the decision, and the reconciliation test that catches the most expensive category of error — a deadline that passes unnoticed.

No lease terms are published on this page, and none were supplied. The only lease fact in the brief is "expires on 2030-2035", which is a five-year window rather than a date. A window cannot be entered into a notice calendar, cannot be modelled as a cash-flow trigger, and cannot be used to price remaining term. This is the largest single gap in the workspace. FLAG-U01 →

The notice-calendar reconciliation

An abstract is a set of facts. A notice calendar is the set of actions those facts require, with dates. Reconciling the two is a mechanical test, and it catches the errors that cost the most money — because every one of them is a deadline that arrives whether or not anyone is watching.

The reconciliation test, in five steps

  1. List every date-bearing obligation in the lease — renewal notice windows, option exercise deadlines, expense-reconciliation delivery dates, insurance certificate renewals, and any tenant reporting obligation tied to percentage rent.
  2. For each, compute the action date working backwards from the deadline by the notice period plus a delivery buffer. If the lease requires 180 days' notice, the action date is 180 days plus a safe buffer before the deadline — not the deadline itself.
  3. Compare against the abstract. Every date in the calendar must trace to a clause in the abstract, and every date-bearing clause must appear in the calendar. Anything on one side but not the other is a discrepancy.
  4. Reconcile against the rent schedule. Escalation dates in the calendar must match escalation dates in the abstract. A mismatch means either the abstract or the billing is wrong — and if it is the billing, there may be an arrears issue that a buyer will find in due diligence.
  5. Record the discrepancy, do not resolve it silently. Each mismatch gets an owner, a question, and a date by which it will be answered.

The specific danger in a 2030–2035 window. If a renewal option requires 12 months' notice before expiry, the action date for an option on a 2030 expiry is in 2029. For a 2035 expiry it is 2034. Those are six years apart. Treating the window as a single date means either preparing years too late or committing to a negotiation years too early — and both are expensive. The notice calendar is the only artefact that fixes this, and it cannot be built until the lease is abstracted.

Where the abstract lives, and where it does not

Belongs in the private file

  • The lease document and every amendment
  • Rent ledger and escalation history
  • CAM reconciliations and the tax bills
  • Insurance certificates and policies
  • The completed abstract and notice calendar
  • Tenant correspondence about renewal intent

Never belongs on a public page

  • Any rent figure from this specific lease
  • Tenant contact names, emails, or addresses
  • The parcel address or assessor's number
  • Owner identity, entity name, or guarantor details
  • Store-level sales, if obtained
  • Anything a competitor or a prospective buyer could use against you
