The back-test

Question: Does trailing appreciation predict forward appreciation in this metro?

Design: Split the 47 quarterly observations into overlapping pairs: trailing 5-year CAGR versus the following 5-year CAGR. 7 usable pairs.

Interpretation. Strongly NEGATIVE correlation. When the metro had been appreciating fast, the next five years were slower, and vice versa. Last five years 4.57%, next five whatever — extrapolation of the trailing rate is not merely unreliable here, it is systematically wrong in the same direction.

Honest limitations
  • Seven pairs is a very small sample. A 100% direction hit rate on seven observations is suggestive, not proof.
  • The index is metro-wide and weighted toward residential transactions. It cannot price a QSR pad and must not be used as the property's appreciation rate.
  • Overlapping windows are not independent, which overstates the apparent statistical strength.
  • The sample covers one interest-rate regime ending at 4.91% on the 10-year. A different rate regime could break the pattern.

Conclusion. The macro engine passes its back-test for the purpose it is actually used for: refusing to extrapolate. It does not, and cannot, produce a property-level forecast.

Cash-flow engine

Annual periods from the valuation date to a terminal year, with a separate terminal value block.

  • Contract period. Valuation date to lease expiration. Rent is contractual; the only uncertainty is tenant default.
  • Option period. Each renewal option, exercised or not, modelled as a probability-weighted branch rather than a single path.
  • Post-expiry. Vacancy, re-leasing cost, then market rent. This is where the hold case is usually weakest and where most simple models cheat by assuming instant re-lease at a higher rent.

Terminal value. NOI at exit divided by an exit cap rate taken from a real quote (Q-01/Q-02). No exit cap rate is assumed here.

Refusal conditions — the model will not answer without these

  • Exit cap rate missing → do not compute terminal value.
  • Discount rate missing → do not compute present value.
  • Lease expiration date missing → do not compute the contract period.
  • Insurance premium missing → compute, but stamp the output 'insurance unverified' because it is a top-three expense line.

Black-swan and base-case stress

The dangerous case is not any single hazard. It is the compound case: the building is damaged (repairs), the tenant cannot operate (rent loss), and every other owner in the submarket is trying to sell at the same time (exit liquidity gone). Each of the three is survivable; together they force a sale into the worst possible market. This module therefore models the joint case as the primary stress, not the union of separate shocks.
