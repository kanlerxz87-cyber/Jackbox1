What each hazard does to the decision

Earthquake ground shaking — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: USGS states that updates are complete for the 2023 National Seismic Hazard Model covering all 50 states. The Toolbox provides web applications for querying and computing hazard from the national models.

Loss channels: Repair / rebuild cost, Business interruption and loss of rent, Deductible and insurance availability, Exit liquidity after a regional event

Evidence needed:

  • Seismic retrofit or structural report by a licensed engineer (ADVISER-2)
  • Current property insurance declarations page incl. earthquake coverage and deductible
  • If the building is masonry-bearing-wall, the local retrofit ordinance timeline

Source: U.S. Geological Survey, National Seismic Hazard Model (2023 50-state update)

Riverine and coastal flooding (NFIP flood hazard) — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: FEMA states flood maps are continually updated and that effective information may change or be superseded. A Flood Insurance Rate Map panel and effective date must be captured, not just a zone letter.

Loss channels: Mandatory flood insurance cost, Rebuild constraints, Lender appetite at sale

Evidence needed:

  • FIRM panel number, zone and effective date for the parcel
  • Flood insurance quote or declaration
  • Any Letters of Map Amendment on file

Source: FEMA, Flood Map Service Center — 'the official public source for flood hazard information produced in support of the National Flood Insurance Program'

Wildfire (Fire Hazard Severity Zone) — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: Zones are Moderate, High or Very High, in State Responsibility Areas (SRA) and now Local Responsibility Area (LRA). SRA maps effective 1 April 2024. OSFM states that FHSZ maps evaluate 'hazard', not 'risk'. A 2026 SRA update is out for public comment from 9 November 2026 to 8 January 2027, so the current designation may change during the holding period.

Loss channels: Insurance non-renewal or repricing, Disclosure on sale, Utility power shut-off affecting a drive-thru

Evidence needed:

  • FHSZ designation from the OSFM viewer, with the date read
  • Confirmation of SRA vs LRA responsibility
  • Note on the pending 2026 SRA update

Source: CAL FIRE — Office of the State Fire Marshal, Fire Hazard Severity Zones

Surface fault rupture — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: The CGS deep links attempted during this session returned the Department of Conservation 'Page Not Found' page, so the specific Alquist-Priolo page could not be confirmed. The programme itself is statutory and the CGS site root resolves. Recorded as an irregularity rather than asserted.

Loss channels: Rebuild prohibition without investigation, Schedule risk on remodel

Evidence needed:

  • Quadrangle name and Alquist-Priolo zone status
  • Any existing geologic report in the lease file

Source: California Geological Survey, Alquist-Priolo Earthquake Fault Zoning Act regulatory zones

Composite multi-hazard risk score — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: FEMA states the National Risk Index 'was created to help illustrate the United States communities most at risk for 18 natural hazards', and that the v1.20 update is available in RAPT. FEMA also publishes a data user guide.

Loss channels: Context only

Evidence needed:

  • Census tract NRI score, expected annual loss and social vulnerability, with the data vintage read from RAPT

Source: FEMA, National Risk Index (v1.20 data update, published through the Resilience Analysis and Planning Tool)

Property-tax relief after a disaster (recovery mechanism, not a hazard) — what it does to the decision

Programme confirmed: The Assessor publishes a Disaster Relief page and a Misfortune & Calamity claim-status tool, and states that owners whose property was damaged by a natural disaster may be eligible for property tax relief.

Loss channels: Partial offset of assessed value after damage

Evidence needed:

  • Whether any prior M&C claim exists on the parcel
  • The filing window that would apply after an event

Source: Los Angeles County Assessor, Disaster Relief and Misfortune & Calamity claims

Black swan framing

What the official programmes actually measure

The Office of the State Fire Marshal is explicit that fire hazard severity zone maps evaluate hazard, not risk, and that hazard is based on physical conditions over a 30 to 50 year period without considering mitigation. FEMA describes flood maps as continually updated, with effective information that may be superseded. Neither programme tells you what happens to this building; they tell you what the ground and the water are like.

Sources: CAL FIRE OSFM · FEMA Flood Map Service Center

What a black swan does to a net-lease asset specifically

The building is small: the franchisor's own prototype is roughly 1,372 sq ft on a drive-thru pad, so repair cost is modest relative to land value. The exposure is therefore not principally rebuild cost. It is the three things that surround it: the months of lost rent while the site is closed, the premium and availability of insurance afterwards, and whether a buyer will still pay a short-lease cap rate for a site in a district that has just been through a regional event. Those three are what the calculator stresses.
