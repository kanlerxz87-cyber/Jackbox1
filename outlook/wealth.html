W-01Basis of inherited propertyGenerally the basis of inherited property is its fair market value at the date of death (or the alternate valuation date).
“Generally, the basis of property inherited from a decedent is one of the following. 1. The FMV of the property at the date of the individual's death.”		Can reset unrealized gain for heirs if the property is held until death. W-02California community propertyIn community property states, including California, both halves of community property generally take the new basis when one spouse dies.
“In community property states (Arizona, California, Idaho, Louisiana, Nevada, New Mexico, Texas, Washington, and Wisconsin) ... When either spouse dies, the total value of the community property, even the part belonging to the surviving spouse, generally becomes the basis of the entire property.”		Whether the property is community property is a title and marital fact, unknown here.observationOL-S21 opened
W-03One-year gift-back exceptionThe basis rule does not apply to appreciated property given to a decedent within one year before death by the person who then receives it back.
“The above rule doesn't apply to appreciated property you receive from a decedent if you or your spouse originally gave the property to the decedent within 1 year before the decedent's death.”		A planning trap to avoid, not a strategy.observationOL-S21 opened

W-05Proposition 19 and commercial propertyThe parent-child reassessment exclusion applies only to a family home or family farm; transfer of commercial property is reassessed.
“the transfer will be subject to reassessment because a commercial property does not qualify for the Proposition 19 intergenerational transfer exclusion, which applies only to the transfer of a family home or family farm.”		Heirs of a directly held commercial parcel should plan for a reset assessed value. Treatment of transfers through an entity is not verified here.observationOL-S24 excerpt
W-06Like-kind exchange (Section 1031)Since 2018 Section 1031 applies only to real property. Cash or other property received ('boot') is taxed to the extent of the gain.
“Section 1031 now applies only to exchanges of real property and not to exchanges of personal or intangible property.”		Allows deferral when selling and reinvesting into real property. Timelines and rules must be confirmed on IRS forms and instructions.observationOL-S22 opened
W-07Installment method and depreciation recaptureDepreciation recapture income is reported in the year of sale even when payments arrive later.
“you must report any depreciation recapture income in the year of sale, whether or not an installment payment was received that year.”		An installment sale defers only the part of the gain above recapture, so cash taxes in the sale year can still be material.observationOL-S23 excerpt
