What this means for a landlord

The estate is shrinking

Franchise units fell from 2,044 to 1,986 over two years — a net loss of 58 units, about 2.8%. Company-operated units were flat at 150 across FY2024 and FY2025. A tenant that is closing units is a tenant whose renewal committee is asking whether a given site earns its place.

Sales, not just closures

Royalties are charged as a percentage of franchisee gross sales. They fell from 207,064 to 189,646, or 8.4%, in two years. Units fell only 2.8%, so royalties are falling faster than the estate — meaning per-unit sales are also under pressure. That is the more alarming of the two series.

The tenant is also a landlord

Jack in the Box Inc. books 332,735 of franchise rental revenue: it owns sites, leases them to franchisees, and subleases others where it is the master lessee. Its weighted-average operating lease term is 11.2 years. So the party across the table from the owner may be the company as master lessee rather than the operating franchisee — which changes who has to be persuaded. Confirm against the lease (Q-05).

Strategic direction is asset-light

In December 2025 the company sold Del Taco for approximately $119 million, described in its own 8-K as a step in a plan to “strengthen the balance sheet and accelerate its shift toward a simpler, asset-light business model.” For a landlord, that is a signal that the company prefers franchised structures to owned real estate — and that the renewal case should be made to the franchisee operator on unit economics, not to the parent.

Careful with the comparison FY2023 reported consolidated figures on a different basis from FY2025 — Del Taco was still consolidated, and a 209,556 impairment charge sits inside the FY2025 segment table. The direction across the three years is unambiguous; individual ratios across years should be recomputed from the filings rather than lifted from any summary, including this one.
