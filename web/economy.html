Economy & trade area

Geography, income levels, school systems, population, job growth, business activity, traffic and every officially verifiable factor influencing the local economy — with regional figures kept alongside blank trade-area rows.

W-01 status: trade-area figures are blank. City/county rows below are explicitly labelled proxies and cannot be substituted for the parcel's trade area. "Central Los Angeles" is not a Census geography; a fixed tract set must be selected in a secure workflow.

Findings & analysis

Population. Two official series point the same direction: California DOF E-1 reports provisional 1/1/2026 population of 9,837,286 for the county (−0.6%) and 3,806,201 for the city (−0.9%); Census QuickFacts V2025 shows a −3.2% county / −0.8% city change from the 2020 estimates base to July 1, 2025. These are different vintages and are not comparable to each other.

Income & housing. County median household income ($90,112, 2020–2024, 2024 dollars) exceeds the city ($81,939), while city median owner-occupied value is higher ($921,200 vs $834,200). These are proxies; trade-area figures remain blank.

Jobs & local economy. EDD reports October 2026 county nonfarm employment at 4,662,800 (preliminary), up 40,700 or 0.9% year over year. Food services and drinking places added 7,500 jobs, while information lost 2,600. Leisure and hospitality added 10,500. BLS shows the metropolitan-division unemployment rate falling from 5.2% in May 2026 to 4.6% (preliminary) in October 2026, and CPI-U 12-month change of 3.7% in September 2026. The sector mix matters more to a quick-service site than the headline rate.

Schools, traffic, competitors. CDE reports 497,105 students in Los Angeles Unified in 2025–26; Census reports a 30.4-minute county mean commute; Caltrans publishes 2021–2024 AADT files for the State Highway System only. Parcel catchment, frontage AADT and competitor counts are blank until the tract set and road are fixed.

Projection system (deep research). SCAG's adopted Connect SoCal 2024 forecast is used as the official long-range projection, not as a prediction: regional employment 8,976,000 (2019) → 10,276,000 (2050); baseline population 20.8 million in 2050 with a 19.85–21.9 million uncertainty range. SCAG explicitly states that subregional forecasting has higher uncertainty than regional forecasting. Observations and assumptions are separated in every table.
