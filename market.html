What the filings support

  • The system is large and almost entirely franchised, so the brand's balance sheet is not the same thing as the tenant's balance sheet. The lease, not the ticker, tells you who pays.
  • The franchisor has publicly disclosed both a closure programme and a strategic review of its second brand. Neither names this unit, but together they indicate a system being actively pruned rather than expanded without constraint.
  • The disclosed site-selection criteria are the agenda for a renewal negotiation. Traffic, access, competition and parking are the four the owner can actually influence or evidence.

What the filings cannot support

  • They cannot tell you this unit's sales, rent, term or renewal intent.
  • They cannot tell you whether this unit is inside the closure programme.
  • They cannot be used to forecast this trade area. A national filing is not a local economic model.

What the evidence actually says

The local economy is improving, unevenly

Metro unemployment fell from 5.5% to 4.4% in twelve months and total nonfarm employment is up 0.5%. That is a gently improving picture, not a boom. Underneath it, the sectors are moving in opposite directions: information lost 5.7% of its jobs while education and health added 3.8%. For a drive-thru, the second number matters more than the first, because hospital and school employment feeds weekday lunch traffic and a stable day-shift population.

What this supports: a hold case that does not assume deterioration. What it does not support: a specific sales forecast for this unit.

Prices are rising slower than the nation, and wages faster

California house prices rose 0.6% over four quarters against 2.5% nationally. Local CPI is running at 3.7% year over year. Meanwhile the Employment Cost Index for wages and salaries in the Los Angeles CSA rose 3.9%, faster than the 3.1% national figure. For a restaurant, that combination is the hard part: revenue indexed to local price growth, labour indexed to something steeper.

What this supports: expecting the tenant to negotiate hard on rent at renewal. What it does not support: concluding the tenant cannot pay.

Two official data gaps affect this page. BLS did not collect October 2025 data during the 2025 lapse in appropriations, so it cannot publish an October 2026 over-the-year change; the 12-month figure shown is September 2026. And the Census county QuickFacts page would not load, so the county benchmark is blank rather than borrowed from a secondary site. Both are on the Flagged for Review page.
